**HR Employee Attrition Analysis Dashboard** >

About the Project

Employee retention is one of the biggest challenges organizations face today. High attrition can increase hiring costs, reduce productivity, and impact overall business performance.

In this project, I analyzed employee data from multiple HR datasets to understand workforce trends, identify factors influencing employee attrition, and provide insights that can help HR teams make better decisions. The final outcome is an interactive dashboard that allows users to explore attrition patterns across departments, demographics, compensation levels, and employee satisfaction metrics.

This project was developed as part of an HR Analytics case study and focuses on transforming raw employee data into meaningful business insights.

---

Business Problem

Organizations often struggle to answer questions such as:

- Which departments experience the highest attrition?
- Does employee income influence retention?
- How does job satisfaction impact employee turnover?
- Are employees leaving due to limited promotion opportunities?
- Which employee groups require more attention from HR teams?

This dashboard was built to help answer these questions through data-driven analysis.

KPIs Analyzed

1. Average Attrition Rate Across Departments

Analyzed employee turnover across different departments to identify areas with higher retention challenges.

2. Average Hourly Rate of Male Research Scientists

Evaluated compensation patterns for a specific employee segment.

3. Attrition Rate vs Monthly Income

Examined the relationship between employee income levels and attrition rates.

4. Average Working Years by Department

Compared employee tenure across departments to understand workforce stability.

5. Job Role vs Work-Life Balance

Analyzed work-life balance ratings across different job roles.

6. Attrition Rate vs Years Since Last Promotion

Investigated whether promotion frequency has an impact on employee retention.

Key Insights

- Attrition varies across departments and employee groups.
- Employee income shows a noticeable relationship with retention trends.
- Job satisfaction levels can significantly influence employee turnover.
- Promotion history may impact an employee's likelihood of leaving the organization.
- Work-life balance differs across job roles and can be an important retention factor.
